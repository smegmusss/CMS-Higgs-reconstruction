# Higgs Boson Mass Reconstruction: Fit & Plotting

This directory contains the Python tools used to analyze, fit, and visualize the invariant mass distribution of the $H \to 4\mu$ candidates generated by the C++ data extraction.

**The Goal of the Fit**
In high-energy physics, a particle's signal appears as a localized peak over a continuous background of other Standard Model processes. Fitting a mathematical model to the data allows us to extract the true mass ($\mu$), the detector resolution ($\sigma$), and effectively isolate the Higgs boson signal from the background noise.

**Tools Used**
* **`numpy`**: For data ingestion from `masses.txt`, histogram generation, and array manipulation.
* **`scipy.optimize.curve_fit`**: To perform the non-linear least-squares minimization.
* **`matplotlib.pyplot`**: To render the final plot, applying Poisson statistical error bars ($\sqrt{N}$) to the data points.

**The Mathematical Model**
The fit function combines two components:
1. **Signal (Gaussian):** Represents the actual Higgs resonance.
   $f(x) = A \cdot \exp\left(-\frac{(x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)$
2. **Background (Linear):** Represents the continuous background events.
   $g(x) = a \cdot x + b$

The algorithm fits the total model: $F(x) = f(x) + g(x)$.

**Isolating the Higgs Peak (Troubleshooting the Z Boson)**
During the analysis, a significant challenge arose due to the physical nature of the dataset:
* **The Z Resonance:** Plotting the full 70-170 GeV mass range revealed a dominant, massive peak at $\approx 91 \text{ GeV}$ corresponding to the $Z$ boson. 
* **Local Minima:** When applying `curve_fit` over the entire range, the algorithm naturally latched onto the massive Z peak to minimize the error, completely washing out the much smaller Higgs signal at 125 GeV.
* **The Solution:** To specifically measure the Higgs boson, we constrained the kinematic window to **110 - 160 GeV**. By cutting out the Z resonance and passing strict physical boundaries (`bounds`) to the initial parameter guesses, we forced the algorithm to correctly identify and fit the Higgs peak at $\approx 125 \text{ GeV}$.
